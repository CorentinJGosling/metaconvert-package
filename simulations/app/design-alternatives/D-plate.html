Risk ratio to odds ratio

Converting a reported risk ratio to an odds ratio requires a baseline risk the source study usually does not give. This study varies the true baseline risk and the value assumed in its place, and asks what the substitution costs.

What the numbers say

Mean RMSE is smallest for dipietrantonj (0.903) and largest for vanderweele_rr_squared (1.753). The other three fall between them, within 0.017 of one another. These aggregates record no Monte Carlo standard error for RMSE, so that middle ordering cannot be separated from simulation noise.

RMSE and bias disagree. vanderweele_rr_squared has the smallest mean absolute bias of the five (0.292) and the largest RMSE, because its replication-to-replication variability is about twice everyone else’s. Closer on average, much less dependable on any one study.

Coverage between 0.992 and 1.000 is over-coverage, not calibration. The SE ratio is 1.77 to 2.27 for every route and mean interval width runs 5.01 to 12.87 on the log odds-ratio scale. The programme records an over-wide standard error across both binary-outcome studies — including transpose, which only copies the log odds-ratio standard error through — names the continuity correction as one candidate, and has not chased it. The correction rates opposite are consistent with that candidate but do not establish it.

About this selection

The misspecification is switched off. The assumed baseline risk equals the true one (br_guess = br = 0.01), so nothing in this view is misspecified — and the substitution cost this study exists to measure is not being exercised. Change br_guess above to see it.

This is the sparsest corner. br = 0.01 is the smallest baseline risk in the grid. At n = 50 the whole 2×2 table holds 0.31 to 0.75 expected cases, so under the generating model the Haldane–Anscombe correction applies to 91%–99% of replications there, and to 26%–76% at n = 300. Much of what is compared here is the behaviour of that correction.

No replication was non-estimable here. Across the whole of study 05, grant returns no estimate in up to 62% of a cell, in the region where rr × br_guess ≥ 1. That region is not reachable from the current selection.

Choosing a measure

Bias describes the point estimate; RMSE adds the estimator’s variability; the SE ratio asks whether the reported standard error matches the real one; coverage is the only measure that degrades under either a wrong estimate or a wrong standard error. No one of them is sufficient, so the table prints them together and the selected measure only sets the order.

Against a same-sample target, coverage and the SE ratio are not interpretable — a nominal 95% interval is not built to cover a quantity recomputed on each replication’s own data — and are reported as unavailable rather than printed.
