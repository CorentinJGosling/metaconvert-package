05 · risk ratio to odds ratio

Converting a reported risk ratio to an odds ratio requires a baseline risk the source study usually does not give. This study varies the true baseline risk and the value assumed in its place, and asks what the substitution costs.

reading

rmse carries no Monte Carlo standard error in these aggregates — bias, emp_se, se_ratio and coverage do. The gap of 0.017 separating transpose, grant and metaumbrella therefore cannot be tested against simulation noise from this measure.

rmse and bias disagree. vanderweele_rr_squared has the smallest mean absolute bias of the five and the largest rmse, because its empirical standard error is about twice everyone else’s. Closer on average, far less dependable on any single study.

!
Coverage near 1.00 is over-coverage, not calibration. se_ratio is 1.77 to 2.27 across all five routes and mean interval width runs 5.01 to 12.87 on the log odds-ratio scale. The programme records an over-wide standard error across both binary-outcome studies — including transpose, which only copies logor_se through — names the continuity correction as one candidate, and has not chased it. The correction rates above are consistent with that candidate but do not establish it.
