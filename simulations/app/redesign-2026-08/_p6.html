Findingcoverage
  1. Use viechtbauer. Mean coverage 0.940 against a nominal 0.95, worst cell 0.916. lipsey_cooper averages 0.764. The 0.176 gap is 11× the 0.016 resolution limit, so it is not simulation noise.
  2. lipsey_cooper is safe to about ρ = 0.25 and then collapses, and it collapses faster the larger the sample: at ρ = 0.75, coverage runs 0.845, 0.457, 0.085, 0.000 across n = 25, 50, 100, 300. Figure 1, right panel.
  3. Neither route miscomputes anything. Against its own estimand each has mean absolute bias under 0.005 and covers at 0.940. The shortfall in 1 and 2 is entirely a difference of estimand: lipsey_cooper returns the point-biserial correlation, this query asks for the biserial, and a larger n narrows the interval around a centre that was never the target.
  4. The ranking reverses if the query does. Against the point-biserial parameter, mean coverage is 0.940 for lipsey_cooper and 0.803 for viechtbauer. Choose the route from which correlation the review pools, not from line 1.
