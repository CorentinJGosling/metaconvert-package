Three directions for the results viewer

Static mockups, not the running app. All three show the same view of the same study — 01a, interval coverage against the biserial correlation — so they can be compared on structure and register rather than on content.

What each one has to fix

“the cards are very obscure”

A number needs a claim attached

Three headline cards currently report a mean deviation, a worst cell and a spread, in that order, and none of them says what to do. All three directions open with a recommendation in a sentence and put the arithmetic under it.

“the left panel mixes design for forest with key scientific questions”

Two kinds of control, two places

Choosing the reference quantity changes the result. Choosing the horizontal axis changes the drawing. They sit in one 340 px scroll today. Each direction separates them, and they differ in where the scientific choices go.

“no users understand the estimand check tab”

It is the study, not a tab

For study 01a the estimand gap is the entire finding: both routes are near-exact, they just estimate different correlations. Buried in tab three, that reads as a diagnostic. Promoted, it is the answer.

Every number below is measured

All figures come from data/aggregated/01a_smd_to_cor_r_nrep1000.csv for the view the app opens on: target = biserial population parameter, p_exp = 0.30, 20 conditions (5 ρ × 4 n), 1,000 replications each. The figure is rendered from that same slice by make-figures.R. Nothing was invented to fill a layout, so every claim in the copy can be checked.

Writing them turned up something the current design hides

The headline card names viechtbauer the leader on coverage. Change the Scored against dropdown to the point-biserial parameter and the ranking reverses exactly: mean coverage goes from 0.940 / 0.764 to 0.803 / 0.940. Scored against its own estimand each route is near-exact — mean absolute bias 0.004 and 0.005. Neither route is better. They estimate different correlations, and which one you want is a property of the review, not of the package. A layout that promotes a leaderboard above the reference quantity that produced it is reporting a dropdown setting as a finding. All three directions below are built so that cannot happen.

Direction

No rail at all. The scientific choices become one sentence across the top, so what you are asking is always legible as a sentence. The plotting controls move down beside the figure they redraw. The page reads recommendation → where it stops holding → why → evidence, and the signature is the boundary grid: every cell is a measured coverage value, tinted by its distance from nominal.

The question

Study 01a — SMD to correlation (r) asks which conversion convert_df() should take. Score the routes against the biserial correlation, population and read confidence interval coverage.

Use viechtbauer.

Over the 20 conditions in this view its intervals cover the biserial correlation 94.0% of the time against a nominal 95%. lipsey_cooper covers 76.4%.

The 0.176 gap between them is 11× the resolution limit of 0.016, the point below which 1,000 replications cannot separate two routes. It is a real difference, not simulation noise.

Read the rows, not the average. lipsey_cooper holds to about ρ = 0.25 and then degrades.

It degrades faster as the sample grows: at ρ = 0.75 coverage falls 0.845 → 0.457 → 0.085 → 0.000 across n = 25 to 300. A larger study is not a safer one here.

That direction is the signature of bias rather than variance, which the next section confirms.

Is that quantity the one you asked for?

For one of them, no. lipsey_cooper returns the point-biserial correlation and this view asks for the biserial. The whole shortfall above is that difference, and a larger n narrows the interval around a centre that was never the target.
